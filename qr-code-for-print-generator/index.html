About this Print-Ready

Print punishes sloppy QR codes in specific, predictable ways: ink spreads (dot gain) and fattens modules, rich-black mixes misregister, and designers crop the quiet zone for style. A print-ready workflow defends against all three — high-resolution source (1024 px here), pure black on white for single-colour work, the 4-module quiet zone kept empty, and a press-proof scanned before the run.

If a brand colour must be used, keep it dark (navy, deep green, burgundy scan; yellow and pastels do not), keep the background lighter than the code by a wide margin, and never invert the code (light pattern on dark ground fails on many scanners).

Best uses

  • Flyers and brochures
  • Packaging and labels
  • Business cards and stationery
  • Signage and exhibition graphics

Put the code exactly where the decision is made — on the table, the counter, the packaging, the invitation or the screen — and give people one short line of instruction such as “Scan to open” or “Scan to pay”. Print codes at least 2 cm wide with a quiet white border and strong contrast so older phone cameras read them quickly; for posters scanned from a distance, use the 1024 px size and print bigger — a good guide is roughly one tenth of the scanning distance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this Print-Ready generator free?

Yes. Loona’s Print-Ready generator is completely free with no signup, no watermark and no limit on how many codes you create, for personal or commercial use.

Will my QR code expire?

No. Loona creates static QR codes: your information is encoded directly inside the QR pattern itself, not stored on a server or hidden behind a paid redirect. A code you print today will keep working as long as the destination itself stays active.

Is my information private on this Print-Ready page?

Yes. Generation happens 100% in your browser using JavaScript. Nothing you enter is sent to or stored on any server.

PNG or vector for print?

At 1024 px, PNG is sharp for codes up to roughly 15 cm. For larger work, ask for the code to be re-rendered at size rather than upscaled, and keep this page’s proportions and quiet zone intact.

Can I print the code in my brand colour?

A dark brand colour on white usually scans well. Test the proof with several phones, including an older Android — that is the strictest judge.
