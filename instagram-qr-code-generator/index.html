Best uses of a Instagram QR Code

In every case the rule is the same: put the QR code exactly where the decision is made — on the table, the counter, the packaging, the invitation or the screen — and give people one short line of instruction such as “Scan to connect” or “Scan to pay”. Codes should be printed at least 2 cm wide, with a quiet white border around them and strong contrast between the QR colour and the background, so older phone cameras can read them quickly. For posters that are scanned from a distance, use the 1024 px size and print the code bigger: a good guide is roughly one tenth of the scanning distance.

Frequently asked questions

Is this Instagram QR code generator free?

Yes. It is completely free, with no signup and no watermark, and you can create codes for as many profiles as you manage.

Does the QR code expire?

No. It is a static code containing your profile address, so it keeps working for as long as that address is live. One thing to remember: if you change your Instagram username, the old address stops working, so generate a fresh code with the new username.

Does the person scanning need the Instagram app?

No. Any modern phone camera can read the code. The profile opens in the Instagram app if it is installed; otherwise it opens in the phone's web browser, where the person can still view a public profile.

My profile is private — what will people see?

They will reach your profile page and can send a follow request, but your photos and videos stay hidden until you accept them. If the code is for a business or creator account that wants open reach, a public profile works best with a printed QR.

Where should I print my Instagram QR code?

Menus and table tents, shop windows, product packaging, receipts, business cards and market-stall banners all work well. Print it a few centimetres across with strong contrast, and test-scan the printed copy once before printing in quantity.
