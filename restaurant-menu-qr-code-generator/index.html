About this Restaurant Menu QR Code

A menu QR lets diners open your full menu on their own phone: hygienic, always up to date, and free to change whenever prices change.

A cafe updates prices on its menu page once; every table QR instantly shows the new menu, no reprinting. That is the real problem a Restaurant Menu QR Code solves: it removes typing, searching and mistakes, and replaces them with a single scan that takes about two seconds. Best uses of a Restaurant Menu QR Code

  • Restaurants, cafes and food trucks
  • Table tents and window stickers
  • Takeaway and delivery menus
  • Hotel room-service menus

In every case the rule is the same: put the QR code exactly where the decision is made — on the table, the counter, the packaging, the invitation or the screen — and give people one short line of instruction such as “Scan to connect” or “Scan to pay”. Codes should be printed at least 2 cm wide, with a quiet white border around them and strong contrast between the QR colour and the background, so older phone cameras can read them quickly. For posters that are scanned from a distance, use the 1024 px size and print the code bigger: a good guide is roughly one tenth of the scanning distance.

Frequently asked questions

Is a web-page menu or a PDF menu better?

A web page usually loads faster, reads better on small phone screens and is easier to edit. A PDF works fine if that is what you have — just keep the file small so it opens quickly, and keep it at the same link when you update it.

Do diners need an app or internet to view the menu?

They need a phone camera — built into almost all phones now — and an internet connection to load the page. No special app, account or sign-up is involved.

One code for every table, or a different code per table?

One code is enough: the same link can be printed on every table tent, the window and takeaway bags. Per-table codes are only needed by ordering systems that track individual tables, which a simple menu link does not do.

How big should the table code be printed?

Around 3–4 cm across, with a clear white border and a short line such as “Scan for the menu”. Table distance is short, so a modest size scans instantly — and test it with your own phone under your restaurant’s lighting before printing the full set.

What about guests without smartphones?

Keep a few printed menus at the counter as a backup. Many restaurants run both: QR codes on the tables for speed, and paper copies for anyone who prefers them.

Can I link to a delivery or ordering page instead?

Yes — the form accepts any menu-style link, so a page that combines your menu with online ordering works too. Just make sure the page opens well on a phone.
