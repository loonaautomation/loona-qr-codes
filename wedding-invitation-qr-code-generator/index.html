About this Wedding Invitation

Printed invitations cannot be updated, but a wedding website can — that is the whole point of putting a QR code on the invitation. Guests scan once and land on the RSVP form, the venue directions and the day’s schedule, and you keep editing those pages until the morning of the wedding.

Keep the code generous in size (at least 2.5 cm on a card), test it against the final card stock — metallic foils and dark backgrounds are the two classic scan killers — and add one line of guidance for older guests: “Point your phone camera at the square to RSVP.”

Best uses

  • RSVP forms on the invitation card
  • Venue directions on the envelope insert
  • Gift registry links
  • Photo-sharing album after the wedding

Put the code exactly where the decision is made — on the table, the counter, the packaging, the invitation or the screen — and give people one short line of instruction such as “Scan to open” or “Scan to pay”. Print codes at least 2 cm wide with a quiet white border and strong contrast so older phone cameras read them quickly; for posters scanned from a distance, use the 1024 px size and print bigger — a good guide is roughly one tenth of the scanning distance.

How it works

The code simply carries your wedding-site or RSVP link as a static URL QR, so it never expires and works with any wedding platform — or a free Google Form for the RSVP.

Worked example

A couple prints the code on the back of their invitation with the words “Scan to RSVP by 1 March”. Replies arrive in a tidy form instead of forty phone calls, and the venue map link saves the usual day-of phone directions.
