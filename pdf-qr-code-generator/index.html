About this PDF QR Code

A PDF QR points to a PDF hosted online, so one scan delivers a document that would be costly or impossible to print for everyone.

An electronics shop puts a PDF QR on the box; the full manual opens on the customer's phone. That is the real problem a PDF QR Code solves: it removes typing, searching and mistakes, and replaces them with a single scan that takes about two seconds. On Loona, the code is generated locally in your browser as a static QR, which means the data lives inside the pattern itself. There is no Loona server in the middle, no short link that can be switched off, and no trial period after which your printed material stops working — a common and expensive problem with many so-called free QR websites.

Best uses of a PDF QR Code

  • Brochures, catalogues and price lists
  • CVs, certificates and reports
  • Study material and e-books
  • Manuals and warranty documents

In every case the rule is the same: put the QR code exactly where the decision is made — on the table, the counter, the packaging, the invitation or the screen — and give people one short line of instruction such as “Scan to connect” or “Scan to pay”. Codes should be printed at least 2 cm wide, with a quiet white border around them and strong contrast between the QR colour and the background, so older phone cameras can read them quickly. For posters that are scanned from a distance, use the 1024 px size and print the code bigger: a good guide is roughly one tenth of the scanning distance.

You can use Loona QR codes freely for commercial work — menus, business cards, client projects, packaging and signage — with no attribution required. Because generation is private and offline-capable once the page has loaded, sensitive details such as WiFi passwords and payment numbers never leave your device. If your information changes later, remember that a static QR cannot be edited after printing, so generate a fresh code and replace the old print; the benefit of that trade-off is a code that can never be held hostage, expire, or stop working because a company closed down.
