About this QR Code Size Calculator

Every failed poster, banner and billboard QR code fails the same way: it was printed too small for the distance it is scanned from. The physics is simple — a phone camera needs each module (the smallest square of the pattern) to cover enough pixels — and the industry rule that captures it is 10:1: the code should be at least one tenth of the scanning distance.

This calculator starts from that rule and adjusts for the two things that change it: content density (a vCard’s 45-module pattern needs more size than a short link’s 25) and conditions (sun glare and hurried scanning both demand margin). It also gives the minimum pixel resolution to download and the quiet-zone width to protect.

Best uses

  • Posters and billboards
  • Packaging and labels
  • Business cards and flyers
  • Signage and exhibition graphics

Put the code exactly where the decision is made — on the table, the counter, the packaging, the invitation or the screen — and give people one short line of instruction such as “Scan to open” or “Scan to pay”. Print codes at least 2 cm wide with a quiet white border and strong contrast so older phone cameras read them quickly; for posters scanned from a distance, use the 1024 px size and print bigger — a good guide is roughly one tenth of the scanning distance.

How it works

Minimum size (cm) = distance (m) × 10 × condition factor × density factor, with a 2 cm floor for hand-held print. The recommended resolution keeps at least ~30 pixels per module at the intended print size, and the quiet zone is four modules of the final code width.

Worked example

A gym poster is read from 3 metres, carrying a normal URL, indoors: 3 × 10 × 1.0 × 1.0 = 30 cm — the calculator’s answer, with a 1024 px download and a 3.6 cm quiet zone. The first poster run at 12 cm scanned only at arm’s length; the reprint at 30 cm works from the door.

What is the absolute minimum size for any QR code?

About 2 × 2 cm for a simple code scanned up close (business cards, packaging). Below that, module size defeats phone cameras — and dense codes like vCards need 2.5–3 cm even at reading distance.

Does the 10:1 rule hold for very long distances?

It is the accepted design rule up to typical signage distances. Beyond ~15 metres, verify with a physical proof at the real distance — atmospheric glare and camera zoom limits start to matter more than geometry.
