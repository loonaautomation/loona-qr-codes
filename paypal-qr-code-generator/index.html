How it works

You enter your PayPal.Me username — or paste your full PayPal.Me link, and the generator trims it down to the username — plus an optional amount. The QR encodes your payment page address: https://www.paypal.com/paypalme/yourusername, with /amount added when you set one. Scanning opens that PayPal page, where the payer sees your name and profile, confirms the amount, and pays from their PayPal account. The money moves through PayPal itself; the code is simply the fastest way to reach your payment page.

Frequently asked questions

What currency is the amount in?

The link carries a plain number with no currency symbol. PayPal presents it in the currency of your PayPal.Me account and shows the payer the amount — and any conversion — before they confirm, so check that your PayPal.Me page shows the currency you expect.

Does the payer need a PayPal account?

In most cases, yes — PayPal.Me payments are sent from a PayPal account, and the page guides the payer through sign-in or sign-up. The exact options vary by country.

I pasted my full PayPal.Me link instead of the username. Will it still work?

Yes. The generator recognises full paypal.me links and reduces them to just the username before building your code.

Can I leave the amount blank?

Yes — the field is optional. Without an amount, your PayPal.Me page opens and the payer types the sum themselves, which suits tips, donations and variable bills.

Can I change the amount after printing?

No — an amount you set becomes part of the encoded link. Generate a new code for a different amount, or print the no-amount version when payments vary.

Are there fees for receiving money this way?

PayPal’s standard fees for receiving payments can apply, depending on the payment type and the countries involved. The QR itself adds no fee, and Loona charges nothing.
