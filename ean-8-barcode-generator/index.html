About this EAN-8

EAN-8 is the short retail barcode for products too small to carry an EAN-13 — lipstick tubes, pens, sachets, individual sweets. It encodes a GTIN-8: unlike EAN-13 it carries no company prefix structure; the whole 7-digit body is issued directly by GS1 because the number space is deliberately scarce.

The final digit is the same modulo-10 GS1 check digit as its bigger sibling, and point-of-sale scanners treat it identically.

Best uses

  • Cosmetics and small toiletries
  • Stationery and confectionery singles
  • Small hardware items
  • Any pack with little label space

Put the code exactly where the decision is made — on the table, the counter, the packaging, the invitation or the screen — and give people one short line of instruction such as “Scan to open” or “Scan to pay”. Print codes at least 2 cm wide with a quiet white border and strong contrast so older phone cameras read them quickly; for posters scanned from a distance, use the 1024 px size and print bigger — a good guide is roughly one tenth of the scanning distance.

How it works

Because GTIN-8 numbers are issued one by one by GS1, they are reserved for genuinely small packs — you cannot derive one from your EAN-13 range. This generator computes or verifies the check digit exactly as a till will.

Worked example

A cosmetics brand launches a 10 ml lip oil. GS1 issues GTIN-8 body 9638507; the generator adds check digit 4 → 96385074, which fits the tiny tube label with room to spare.

Can I just shorten my EAN-13 to make an EAN-8?

No — GTIN-8 is a separate, centrally-issued number space. A truncated EAN-13 will not scan as a valid product.

How small can I print an EAN-8?

GS1 nominal width is about 26 mm at 100% magnification; the standard allows 80–200% scaling. Keep the quiet zones (blank margins) intact.
