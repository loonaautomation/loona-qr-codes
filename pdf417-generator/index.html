About this PDF417

PDF417 (ISO/IEC 15438) is the wide stacked barcode on the back of US driving licences (AAMVA standard), airline boarding passes (the original BCBP format), ID cards and shipping labels. It stores a portable data file — hence the name — of up to ~1,850 characters with strong, adjustable Reed–Solomon error correction, in rows that ordinary 1D-style scanners can read line by line.

Its rectangular shape and row structure make it printable by ordinary label and laser printers, one reason governments adopted it long before camera scanning existed.

Best uses

  • Driving licences and ID cards
  • Boarding passes
  • Government forms and permits
  • Legacy logistics labels

Put the code exactly where the decision is made — on the table, the counter, the packaging, the invitation or the screen — and give people one short line of instruction such as “Scan to open” or “Scan to pay”. Print codes at least 2 cm wide with a quiet white border and strong contrast so older phone cameras read them quickly; for posters scanned from a distance, use the 1024 px size and print bigger — a good guide is roughly one tenth of the scanning distance.

How it works

Generated with the vendored bwip-js encoder at standard compaction. For licence/ID use, the payload must follow the issuing authority’s data layout exactly (AAMVA for US licences) — this generator encodes the text you give it, in order.

Worked example

PDF417 or Data Matrix?

PDF417 when a standard names it (licences, older boarding passes) or when wide labels and line scanners are the constraint. Data Matrix for new industrial/healthcare marking in small spaces.

Can phones scan PDF417?

Yes — many scanner apps and some camera apps read it (it is how licence-scanning apps work), though long symbols need the whole code in frame.
